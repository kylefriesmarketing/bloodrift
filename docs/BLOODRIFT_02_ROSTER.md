## 1. ZENITH — *The Setting Sun*
**Archetype:** All-rounder / shoto · **Difficulty:** ★☆☆

**Identity.** Earth's paragon for thirty years — a man whose cells drink starlight. The Convergence blotted out clean sunlight, so now every miracle burns his own reserves. He is, quietly, dying, and fighting anyway. The face of the Vanguard; the first character new players should touch.

**Look.** Weathered golden armor-suit over scar tissue that glows through the cracks like banked coals. Cape burned to a ragged mantle. When he overdraws, veins of light crawl up his neck and his eyes smoke.

**Playstyle.** Honest fundamentals: great walk speed, a true fireball, an anti-air that feels like justice. His depth is the overdraw economy — any special can be *Flared* (self-charged without meter) at a cost.

**Signature Mechanic — Flare.** Hold the Rift button during any special to supercharge it *without* spending pints — instead it adds to his **Solar Debt** gauge.

**RPG Hook — SOLAR DEBT.** Debt persists *between matches*. Carried debt burns his max HP next match (up to −15%) until paid down by "shedding heat" (win rounds without Flaring, rest nodes in campaign, or the *Coronal* gear line). His tree lets you build **Miser** (debt-averse, efficiency buffs) or **Supernova** (debt-embracing: at max debt his fists gain burn damage and his Executions change visually — he kills like a dying star). Power now, pay later: a hero's arc as a mechanic.

**Key Specials.**
- *Sunlance* (qcf+FP): straight photon bolt; Flared version pierces and ignites.
- *Judgment Rise* (dp+BP): rising uppercut, invincible startup when Flared.
- *Gravity of Duty* (qcb+FK): short dash-through with a backfist — his whiff-punish tool.
- *Daybreak Slam* (air, d+BP): descending double-hammerfist, bounces on counter-hit.
- *Corona Guard* (qcb+BP): parry stance; successful parry refunds 0.5 pint or 1 Debt.

**Sunders.** ARMS: catches a punch and *boils* the forearm in his grip (trigger: parry a punch-tagged attack). LEGS: heel-stomp that shatters a shin against his knee (trigger: punish a blocked low). BODY: point-blank chest flare that X-rays ribs curling from heat (trigger: land Flared Sunlance at max range).

**Overdrive — NOON.** He ignites the arena's sky for one second of true daylight: a screen-wide column of vertical light hammers the opponent into the ground, old glory for one heartbeat. (In Court arenas, the monsters' architecture visibly *recoils*.)

**Executions.**
1. **"Dawn, Once More."** He holds the loser by the collar, whispers "look away," and goes supernova at skin contact — flesh vaporizes in a rolling wave from the touch-point, leaving a standing, smoking skeleton that takes one step and collapses into the blood pool, which *boils*.
2. **"The Long Fall."** He rockets them into the black Rift-sky until both vanish. Three silent seconds. The loser returns alone as a meteor — the crater fills slowly from the bottom with what's left.

**Desecration — "Last Light"** (S-rank Sunlance): a routine Sunlance suddenly burns through instead of knocking down; the opponent stands haloed in light for a beat, then drops as ash from the chest out.

**Rivalries.** NULL (a sun versus the void that eats suns — his debt is literally food to it) · STRIGOI (the Count wants the vintage of a star; Zenith's blood burns going down).

**Gear examples.** *Coronal Weave* (Body, Epic): shed 1 Debt per round won. *Mourning Cape* (Relic, Riftforged): at max Solar Debt, Bleeding you inflict never times out — but your own wounds don't visually close between rounds.

## 2. TRIAGE — *The Combat Surgeon*
**Archetype:** Technical / debuffer · **Difficulty:** ★★★

**Identity.** Dr. Imara Vale ran the Vanguard's trauma ward for a decade — she has put every hero on this roster back together, and she remembers where the seams are. When the Convergence hit, she stopped asking to be protected. Fights with surgical steel, tourniquets, and a medic's unbearable calm; narrates wounds clinically as she inflicts them.

**Look.** Matte-black field scrubs under a webbing harness of instruments; red cross emblem defaced into a jagged X. Headlamp that flicks on for Sunders. Gore accumulates on her gloves *first* — by round 3 she's elbow-deep red and pristine everywhere else.

**Playstyle.** Mid-range scalpel pressure and debuff stacking. Weak raw damage, monstrous *compounding* damage: her gameplan is opening wounds, accelerating Bleed, and letting the Living Blood system be her real combo.

**Signature Mechanic — Operating Theater.** Her attacks apply *Incisions* (stacking bleed intensifiers) to limb regions. The Rift button performs *Rounds* — a quick diagnostic glance that reveals the opponent's limb-damage totals and marks the weakest limb for +damage.

**RPG Hook — ANATOMY ATLAS.** Every limb she Sunders is **permanently charted for that character**, account-wide: future matches against any ZENITH, any GRAFT, start with that limb taking +8% damage from her. Fifteen characters × four regions = a 60-page atlas to complete; a full-charted character unlocks a unique intro ("I've read your file"). Matchup knowledge, literalized as her leveling system.

**Key Specials.**
- *Scalpel Arc* (qcf+FP): flung blade; EX throws three in a fan, each applies an Incision.
- *Rib Spreader* (b,f+BP): lunging hook that latches — success drags her point-blank into her mix.
- *Tourniquet* (qcb+FK): self-cleanse one Bleeding state / EX also refunds meter. Her survival tool.
- *Adrenaline Spike* (dp+FK): syringe uppercut; counter-hit causes a jittering crumple stun.
- *Clamp* (command grab, hcb+Throw): brief anesthetic hold — steals 0.5 pint as "a sample."
- *Pool interaction:* Transfusion breaker costs 1 pint (not 2) while she stands in any blood pool.

**Sunders.** ARMS: severs the biceps tendon with two precise cuts — bone-cam shows the muscle snapping up like a window blind (trigger: punish with Scalpel Arc at max range). LEGS: hamstring slice from a slide (trigger: counter-hit a running opponent). BODY: rib-spreader *used as intended*, on camera (trigger: land Clamp on a Bleeding opponent).

**Overdrive — MALPRACTICE.** She flash-anesthetizes the opponent to their knees and performs eleven procedures in four seconds — the camera cuts to her headlamp POV, all glinting steel and arterial spray — then clicks her penlight off: every limb leaves the cinematic one wound-state worse.

**Executions.**
1. **"Autopsy, Live."** The loser is still standing when she begins the Y-incision. She works fast and gently, narrating findings to an unseen recorder — "cause of death…" — and finishes the sentence by showing them their own heart, still keeping time. It stops. So do they.
2. **"Do No Harm."** She sews every wound on their body shut with blurring speed — the crowd-pleaser reversal: the opponent, fully mended, looks up amazed. "First, do no harm." Then she taps the one Incision she left open over the aorta. The screen goes red from the inside out.

**Desecration — "Second Opinion"** (S-rank Adrenaline Spike): the syringe uppercut lands and the opponent simply doesn't come down whole — the plunger was full of Rift-clot; they crystallize mid-air and shatter across the pool.

**Rivalries.** GRAFT (she's the only one who calls him a *patient*, and she wants her colleagues' limbs back) · VYRM (a parasite wearing a body she once saved — she takes that personally).

**Gear examples.** *Black Bag* (Relic, Epic): start each match with one random enemy limb pre-Incised. *Oath-Breaker's Gloves* (Arms, Riftforged): Executions chart limbs for the Atlas twice as fast, but Spares grant her no XP at all.

## 3. CENTURION IX — *The Ninth Bearer*
**Archetype:** Stance / weapon-swap · **Difficulty:** ★★★

**Identity.** For a century, the Centurion mantle has passed to a new bearer each time the last one falls. Marcus Osei is the ninth — a museum conservator who catalogued the armory and then, one funeral later, had to wear it. Eight ghosts live in the gear. They do not always agree with him, or each other.

**Look.** Composite armor visibly assembled from eight eras — a '20s brass gauntlet, a Cold-War era helm, a jet-age chestplate — each piece faintly haunted (heat-shimmer silhouettes of former bearers flicker during stance swaps). The IX is fresh paint.

**Playstyle.** The loadout character. He carries **three Relics** into battle (chosen pre-match from eight), and each Relic replaces an entire special-move family. Stance-swap on the Rift button cycles them. His identity is preparation: build the right Centurion for the matchup.

**Signature Mechanic — Bearer Stances.** Rift button cycles equipped Relics (e.g., *Relic II — the Lance* = spacing/keepaway family; *Relic V — the Aegis* = shield/armor family; *Relic VII — the Twin Hooks* = grab/trip family). Swaps are combo-cancelable at higher mastery.

**RPG Hook — THE RELIC ARMORY.** Each of the 8 Relics levels **individually** through use, unlocking that dead Centurion's deeper techniques and, at Relic Lv10, their **Echo** — a once-per-match ghost-assist where the previous bearer briefly manifests to extend a combo or take a hit for Marcus. Campaign side-quests recover the Armory's missing pieces and each bearer's story. Collecting and mastering all eight = the "Century" title and his third Execution.

**Key Specials (family samples).**
- *Lance family:* Pilum Toss (qcf+FP), Phalanx Step (advancing thrust with forward armor).
- *Aegis family:* Testudo (hold to armor through one hit), Shield Rush (b,f+BP wallcarry).
- *Twin Hooks family:* Harvest Trip (low grab), Gaff & Drag (hcb+Throw — drags them through the nearest blood pool, which counts as a pool tick for him).
- *Universal:* Standard Bearer (dp+FK) — plants a banner that slowly builds pints while it stands; opponents can break it.

**Sunders.** ARMS: Aegis rim brought down on a braced elbow like a paper-cutter (trigger: armor through a hit, then punish). LEGS: Lance pinned through the foot to the arena floor — bone-cam of the pin (trigger: land Harvest Trip on a dashing opponent). BODY: Twin Hooks set under the collarbones and *pulled* (trigger: throw a cornered opponent).

**Overdrive — THE HONOR GUARD.** All eight prior Centurions manifest in a ring. The opponent is passed around it — each ghost lands their era's signature blow, a century of technique in three seconds — before Marcus finishes with the plain, human right cross he had before any of this.

**Executions.**
1. **"The Tenth Pedestal."** The ghosts hold the loser upright while Marcus plants the Lance through them at parade angle. Stone crawls up from the arena floor, petrifying them mid-defiance — a new statue for the Hall of Bearers. The camera pulls back: there are many, many more than ten pedestals.
2. **"Changing of the Guard."** He fits the brass gauntlet of Centurion I onto his fist, and the ghosts *pile in* — the single punch carries eight overlapping after-images and takes the opponent's torso clean through the arena wall, leaving them folded in the rubble like a closed book.

**Desecration — "Muster"** (S-rank Standard Bearer): opponent is knocked into the planted banner; it un-plants itself in their chest, and the ghosts salute.

**Rivalries.** KHET (two living archives — the pharaoh calls the mantle "a dynasty of one century; adorable") · ORDNANCE (the war-machine has killed three prior Centurions; the Armory remembers each round).

**Gear examples.** *Bearer's Oath* (Body, Epic): start with a second Echo charge, but locked to 2 Relics. *Museum Wax* (Relic, Rare): Relic XP +20%; ghosts visibly linger longer (cosmetic swagger).

## 4. JOULE — *The Pain Bank*
**Archetype:** Grappler / tank · **Difficulty:** ★★☆

**Identity.** Desmond "Dez" Okafor was a demolition foreman buried under forty stories on Convergence Day. The Rift-scar under the rubble made him a deal his body accepted before his mind could refuse: *nothing is lost, everything is stored.* Now every hurt he takes is a deposit. The gentlest man on the roster; hugging him is how you die.

**Look.** Huge, soft-faced, high-vis workwear armored with salvaged I-beam plate. Sub-dermal capacitor scarring across his back glows amber as the Bank fills — at max charge he *hums* audibly and the air around him distorts.

**Playstyle.** Classic grappler with a heretical resource: he *wants* to be hit. Stored joules discharge through command grabs and armored advances. Terrifying comeback structure — the more you beat him, the worse your options get.

**Signature Mechanic — Kinetic Bank.** Damage taken converts to Joules (visible arm-gauge). Spend on: *Discharge* follow-ups to grabs (+damage), *Absolute Armor* (Rift button — eats one full combo, banking it all), or *Ground Fault* (banked wave-quake).

**RPG Hook — THE PAIN BANK.** Unspent Joules **persist between matches** as an account he can carry into the next fight (capped by level). His Dominance Trees monetize suffering differently: **Compound Interest** (persistent bank grows if he *loses* matches — the roster's only mechanic that pays out on defeat, turning losing streaks into a loaded weapon) vs **Full Discharge** (bigger in-match spends, bank resets each match). Campaign rest nodes offer a devil's choice: heal Sunders *or* keep the pain banked.

**Key Specials.**
- *Bear Down* (hcb+Throw): the signature hug — mash to squeeze; Discharge version cracks the screen edges.
- *Wrecking Approach* (b,f+BP): armored walking lariat.
- *Ground Fault* (qcb+FK): banked-energy floor quake, hits both ends of the arena at max Bank.
- *Spot Me* (dp+FP): catches a jump-in out of the air into a powerbomb.
- *Absolute Armor* (Rift): stance — eats everything for 1.5s, banks it. Grabs beat it; that's the dance.

**Sunders.** ARMS: catches a punch in one fist and *closes his hand* (trigger: Absolute Armor absorbs 3+ hits, then punish). LEGS: a stomp through a planted thigh like breaking shuttering timber (trigger: Spot Me a jump-in at max Bank). BODY: the hug, dialed to a Sunder — bone-cam of ribs flexing past tolerance (trigger: land Bear Down twice in one round).

**Overdrive — STRUCTURAL FAILURE.** He opens the Bank all the way: grabs the opponent and releases every stored newton *through* them into the arena — the background visibly demolishes in a controlled-implosion wave, and the opponent is the charge.

**Executions.**
1. **"Return to Sender."** He taps the loser's chest with two fingers, almost kind — and discharges the entire Bank at once. The camera stays on his sad face while, behind him, the silhouette leaves through the arena wall in a cone of red. He hard-hats the dust off. "Nothing personal. It was yours."
2. **"Load-Bearing."** He hoists them overhead like a beam, walks to the Rift-scar, and *presses* — the bone-cam shows the spine accepting a building's worth of stored weight for exactly one frame. What he sets down, gently, is not load-bearing anymore.
*(Spare variant — unique to JOULE: choosing Mercy, he absorbs the loser's Bleeding wounds into his own Bank. It hurts him. He does it anyway. Unlocks the "Good Neighbor" tree node.)*

**Desecration — "Overtime"** (S-rank Bear Down): the hug simply doesn't stop at the round-end bell; the freeze-frame is the crowd flinching.

**Rivalries.** LYCAON (the wolf can't understand prey that thanks you for the bite) · VESPRA (her brood's thousand stings are a savings plan as far as Dez is concerned).

**Gear examples.** *Foreman's Ledger* (Relic, Epic): +15% Bank conversion while below half health. *Site-Safety Vest, Cursed* (Body, Riftforged): begin every match with 20% missing HP, pre-banked as Joules.

## 5. MARROW — *The Ossuary*
**Archetype:** Zoner / crafter · **Difficulty:** ★★☆

**Identity.** Sera Voss's power was voted "least marketable" by the hero networks: total command of bone — hers, and eventually, everyone's. The Convergence made the ghouls' aesthetic mainstream and Sera stopped apologizing. She grows her arsenal from her own skeleton, harvests better materials from the fallen, and considers herself the Vanguard's only honest weapons program.

**Look.** Pale armor of overlapping grown-bone plates in elegant, cathedral-like patterns — beauty is the point. Regrowth glows faint white at her forearms where she draws new spears. Her cloak is wired with vertebrae trophies (see RPG hook), and yes, they're labeled.

**Playstyle.** Premier zoner: bone spears, floor-spike zones, wall construction. Ammo economy — drawing from her own skeleton costs a sliver of health (she is literally spending herself), harvested-bone ammo is free. Keepaway that must occasionally get her hands dirty to restock.

**Signature Mechanic — Ossify.** Rift button raises a bone barricade segment (projectile-blocking, breakable). Her spears that *kill* momentum in the barricade can be re-collected. Spent spears remain in the arena as pickups — her zoning leaves architecture.

**RPG Hook — THE OSSUARY.** Executions and Sunders harvest **bone stock by species** — hero, monster, alien bone are different crafting materials (alien chitin-bone makes lighter, faster spears; Court bone holds curses; hero bone hits harder). Between matches she *crafts and upgrades her actual movelist* at the Ossuary bench: spear tips, barricade patterns, armor weight — the roster's crafting economy. Her masterwork recipes require specific donors ("GRAFT femur ×1" — good luck, he reuses everything).

**Key Specials.**
- *Spina* (qcf+FP): bone spear; EX fires a bouquet of three.
- *Reliquary Wall* (Rift): barricade segment; two can stack into a full wall.
- *Vertebral Whip* (b,f+BP): her own extended spine-lash — mid-range poke, drags airborne foes down.
- *Gravefield* (qcb+FK): floor spikes zone, low-hitting, great oki.
- *Calcify* (dp+BP): counter stance — attacker's striking limb is flash-cased in bone (brief limb-specific disable: a taste of a Sunder).

**Sunders.** ARMS: Calcify counter, then she *snaps the cast* (trigger: Calcify a punch-tagged special). LEGS: Gravefield spike through the heel, bone-cam of the spike ascending (trigger: opponent wakes up into Gravefield). BODY: point-blank Spina volley that the camera follows *through* (trigger: land Spina on an opponent bounced off Reliquary Wall).

**Overdrive — CATHEDRAL.** She slams both palms to the floor and grows a ribvault of colossal bone around the opponent — a flying buttress of femurs — then closes it like a fist. It crumbles to reveal them crumpled in a rose window of their own blood.

**Executions.**
1. **"Donor Card."** She extends one open hand; the opponent's skeleton *answers*. It walks them forward one marionette step at a time — bone obeying her, flesh objecting — until the skeleton simply steps out and stands beside her like a new recruit. She catalogs it on a wrist-slate. The rest stays where it fell.
2. **"Iron Maiden."** A gentle push on the sternum, and every bone in the loser's body blooms outward at once — spurs erupting through skin in a perfect, symmetrical iron-maiden lattice. She circles it once, critically, then nods: "Load-bearing. Good bones." It's the only headstone they get.

**Desecration — "Restock"** (S-rank Spina): the final spear pins them to her Reliquary Wall; the freeze-frame is her already walking over to collect her ammunition.

**Rivalries.** HARROW (he collects heads, she collects everything below — there is a professional-courtesy intro where they compare inventories) · KHET (dry old bones with delusions of dynasty; she calls him "materials").

**Gear examples.** *Alien Lattice Tips* (Arms, Epic): Spina speed +15% while alien-bone stocked. *The First Vertebra* (Relic, Riftforged): Vertebral Whip range doubles, but every use applies one Incision-equivalent to her own BODY region — power drawn from her own spine.

## 6. STRIGOI — *The Voivode of Veins*
**Archetype:** Drain rushdown / mid-range · **Difficulty:** ★★☆

**Identity.** The eldest vampire of the Midnight Country, a warlord-aristocrat who has run a blood economy for six hundred years and finds the Rift's arrival *vindicating* — reality itself has adopted his business model. Courtly, patient, and genuinely delighted by the new cuisine: hero blood carries starlight, alien ichor is an acquired taste he is acquiring.

**Look.** Funeral-silk greatcoat over blackened cuirass; pale to translucency, veins visible and *mobile*. No fangs on display until they matter — his silhouette reads noble until the jaw unhinges. Drained blood visibly routes through him, flushing his pallor rose.

**Playstyle.** Mid-range aggression with drain sustain. Every blocked string leaks a little life to him; his pressure is a siphon. Weak health pool balanced by constant recovery — kill him fast or feed him forever.

**Signature Mechanic — Sanguine Draw.** Rift button: short-range tether that sips health and meter from a struck or *pooled* source — he is the premier blood-pool character, drinking arena pools dry to heal (each pool once). His EX moves spend health instead of pints if he chooses — blood is blood.

**RPG Hook — THE BLOOD BANK.** Blood drained in matches banks permanently, *by type*: Hero, Court, and Dominion vintages are separate currencies. Spend at his estate (campaign hub cellar) on permanent boons — but each vintage buys different lines (Hero blood: daylight tolerance, stat boons; Court blood: elder forms — bat-swarm dash, mist backdash; Dominion ichor: the experimental list — glyph-resistances, ichor-adapted drains). His "collect the whole cellar" endgame: drain every roster character to fill the Grand Tasting ledger, unlocking his third Execution and the *Connoisseur* title.

**Key Specials.**
- *Red Line* (b,f+FP): darting palm-strike with tether follow-up.
- *Cask Slam* (hcb+Throw): command grab — a two-fang tap, decorous, devastating; heals him.
- *Vesper Wing* (qcb+FK): cloak-sweep launcher; EX becomes a bat-swarm crossup.
- *Old Mist* (qcb+BP, unlocked via Bank): brief mist-form retreat through the opponent.
- *The Toast* (dp+FP): rising chalice-hand uppercut that catches jump-ins — he *toasts* the falling body.

**Sunders.** ARMS: wrist taken to his mouth mid-parry — the bone-cam is the radial artery emptying in a heartbeat-synced pulse (trigger: parry with Red Line follow-up). LEGS: femoral tap behind the knee, coldly clinical (trigger: Cask Slam a kneeling/waking opponent). BODY: he presses one palm flat to the chest and *withdraws* — the ribcage visibly deflates a size (trigger: drain 3 pools in one match).

**Overdrive — THE RED HARVEST.** He unhinges entirely — greatcoat blooming into a wall of wings — and hits the opponent as a swarm from six angles, each pass a long red thread pulled from them to him, reassembling on the far side flushed, young, and *bored*.

**Executions.**
1. **"Decanted."** He doesn't touch them. One raised hand, a sommelier's twist — and their blood answers him *through the pores*, lifting from the body in a thousand fine threads that braid into a sphere above his palm. The husk drops like laundry. He tastes the vintage, considers, and — highest praise — finishes the glass.
2. **"The Red Cellar."** A blur; the loser is suddenly inverted, ankle-chained to a rafter that wasn't there before, above a single crystal glass. He opens the throat with one thumbnail, then sits in a chair that also wasn't there before, and waits for the glass to fill. The camera holds on the drip. He has *time*.

**Desecration — "Corkage"** (S-rank Cask Slam): the decorous two-fang tap simply continues past the bell — the freeze-frame is his hand raised politely: *a moment, please.*

**Rivalries.** ZENITH (the cellar's white whale: star-fed blood, nearly ash, worth the burns) · FLUX (her Codex rewrites her blood mid-drain — the only vintage that *changes flavor while he drinks*, which offends and fascinates him).

**Gear examples.** *Signet of the First Winter* (Relic, Epic): pools he drinks refund 0.5 pint extra. *Tastevin of the Rift* (Relic, Riftforged): may drain CONFLUX and boss-class blood — unbanks a random vintage each time (the good stuff has interest rates).

## 7. LYCAON — *The First Wolf*
**Archetype:** Transforming rushdown · **Difficulty:** ★★★

**Identity.** The original werewolf — the king cursed by the old gods, who has spent three thousand years perfecting the thing they punished him with. He does not "suffer" the change; he *budgets* it. The Convergence broke the moon's clockwork, and for the first time in millennia his calendar is interesting again.

**Look.** Human form: a lean, scarred huntsman-king in wolf-pelt and bronze-age fragments of his old crown. Beast form: a seven-foot dire-wolf terror that keeps the crown fragments *embedded* — the silhouette must read as royalty in both shapes. Transformation is fast, wet, and audibly skeletal.

**Playstyle.** Two full movesets. Human: disciplined counter-fighter, evasive steps, precision. Beast: the fastest rushdown in the game, chip-heavy, self-damaging frenzy (the form costs him health-over-time). The art is *when* to change.

**Signature Mechanic — The Change.** Rift button transforms (brief vulnerable window; cancelable at high mastery). Beast form runs a Frenzy clock — damage dealt extends it, whiffs shorten it; expiring reverts him exhausted (brief debuff).

**RPG Hook — THE HUNGER MOON.** A real lunar meter advances **one phase per match played** (any mode). At waxing phases he starts matches part-changed with buffs; the full-moon match starts him in Beast form with the Frenzy clock frozen — his god-match, once per cycle. Then it wanes: new-moon matches he is *only human*, Beast locked, forced to win with three-thousand-year-old fundamentals. Skill trees split **The Man** (deepen human form: parries, weapon fragments, new-moon boons) vs **The Beast** (deepen wolf: pack howls, extended Frenzy, gore trails). The player lives on his calendar — planning ranked sessions around his moon is the fantasy.

**Key Specials (per form, samples).**
- Human — *King's Measure* (b,f+FK): step-in cut with the crown-shard knife; *Old Discipline* (qcb+BP): parry that, on success, advances the moon one tick (rewards patience with power).
- Beast — *Gutter Run* (b,f+FK): low lope under highs into rib-bite; *Throat Answer* (dp+FP): leaping anti-air jaws; *Carrion Call* (qcb+FK): howl — brief speed buff, extends Frenzy; near pools, the howl summons two spectral pack-wolves for one crossup pass.
- *Pool interaction:* standing in blood extends the Frenzy clock — the beast is fed by the arena.

**Sunders.** ARMS (human): the counter-fighter takes an over-extended arm and rotates it a scientific, awful amount (trigger: Old Discipline parry at low health). LEGS (beast): ankle taken in jaws, *shaken* (trigger: Gutter Run counter-hit). BODY (beast): both paws through the guard and a bite that the bone-cam frames like a closing bear-trap (trigger: connect Throat Answer during Carrion Call).

**Overdrive — THE HUNT REMEMBERS.** The arena lights die to moonlight. Three passes in darkness — each pass a strobe-lit hit — and on the fourth strobe he is simply *behind* the opponent in human form, letting them fall, resetting his cuffs like a king after a hunt. In Beast form the finisher pose swaps: he howls, and every blood pool in the arena howls back in ripples.

**Executions.**
1. **"The Old Way."** Beast form, if he isn't already. He circles once — the pack-ghosts of every wolf he has ever led materialize and circle with him — and then the screen is all fur and red. When it clears, the pack sits in a patient ring, and he stands at the center over what remains, human again, wiping his mouth with a king's economy. The wolves divide the rest. He always eats first; he never eats alone.
2. **"Wishbone."** One paw on each of the loser's shoulders, jaws taking the collar. A single shake of the head — the bone-cam shows the spine at cervical, then the wide shot completes the thought in silhouette against the broken moon: *make a wish.*

**Desecration — "Short Leash"** (S-rank Gutter Run): the low lope simply doesn't stop at the bell — the freeze-frame is a red streak exiting frame, one boot left standing.

**Rivalries.** JOULE (a prey animal with no fear response is a theological problem for a wolf) · VESPRA (two monarchs of hungry subjects; her brood smells like *competition*).

**Gear examples.** *Crown Fragment: Temporal* (Relic, Epic): moon advances 2 phases on Execution kills — sprint your calendar with cruelty. *The Unbroken Pelt* (Body, Riftforged): Beast form health-drain removed at full moon, doubled at new moon — all-in on the calendar.

## 8. GRAFT — *The Amalgam*
**Archetype:** Grappler / move-thief · **Difficulty:** ★★☆

**Identity.** Not Frankenstein's creature — the tradition it founded. GRAFT built *himself* from the Midnight Country's battlefield dead across two centuries, and he considers a body a rough draft, never a final. The Convergence delivered two new realities of superior parts. He is unfailingly polite about it, which is worse: he treats dismemberment as a fitting.

**Look.** Nine feet of mismatched mastery — a knight's arm, a giant's shoulder, sutures like mountain ranges, iron staples as jewelry. **Playstyle.** Slow, armored grappler with unreactable-throw terror and — uniquely — a stolen-move slot machine that breaks his own archetype. A Graft with ZENITH's fireball is a different matchup than a Graft with LYCAON's lope. The opponent must scout *your* build.

Each stolen move is renamed and *re-fleshed* in his animation set (ZENITH's Sunlance becomes *Borrowed Light*, thrown from a shoulder that still glows). The collection-driven players' character; the doc's clearest "kills mean something" statement.

**Key Specials.**

**Sunders.** ARMS: he removes an opponent's arm at the elbow *with intent*, examines it, and — finding it wanting — uses it as the club for the rest of the cinematic (trigger: land Fitting twice). LEGS: a boot-stomp through the knee with a giant's mass, bone-cam of a joint becoming a hinge the wrong way (trigger: Meat Wall absorbs a full string, then punish). BODY: both hands take the ribcage like a jar lid (trigger: opponent's ARMS already Sundered — he escalates the fitting).

**Overdrive — SECONDHAND.** Every stolen move in his current loadout fires in sequence as a stitched-together combo — other characters' signatures performed on other characters' limbs — ending with all his sutures pulling tight at once, compressing him around the opponent like a closing fist of borrowed muscle.

**Executions.**
1. **"Alterations."** He sets the loser on an iron table that rises from the arena floor (the Court provides), and works with pins in his mouth like a tailor at a deadline — the camera stays discreetly on his face, all craftsman's absorption, while the sounds do the work. He turns their chin left, right. Nods. "Improved."

**Rivalries.** TRIAGE (the only person who stitches better than he does; he has proposed *collaboration*; she has proposed his arrest) · MARROW (a materials dispute of long standing — her masterwork recipe calls for his left femur, which once belonged to a hero she catalogued; they keep receipts).

**Gear examples.** *Surgeon's Enemies List* (Relic, Epic): Harvest thefts also grant +5% graft-limb health each. *The Original Staple* (Relic, Riftforged): a third stolen-move slot; his own base specials −10% damage — less him, more everyone else.

## 9. KHET — *The Ledger of Ages*
**Archetype:** Zoner / curse debuffer · **Difficulty:** ★★★

**Identity.** A pharaoh who conquered death by *accounting* — every soul he owned in life was recorded in the Ledger, and what is recorded, he keeps. Buried four thousand years with his bureaucracy of scarabs; the Convergence cracked the tomb-realm into the Midnight Country's borders and he emerged to find three worlds of unrecorded souls. Delinquent accounts, all of them.

**Look.** Regal desiccation — gold death-mask fused to the skull beneath, wrappings that move independently like a court of snakes, a floating retinue of canopic jars orbiting his shoulders. Sand runs continuously from his joints; he never stops slightly *pouring*.

**Playstyle.** Full-screen bureaucracy: sand geysers, wrapping-snares, scarab swarms, curse stacking. The roster's hardest keepaway; touching KHET should feel like an achievement. His damage arrives late, itemized, with interest.

His progression tree grows the Ledger's terms (deeper starting curses, interest on repeat opponents) or forgives it for power elsewhere. **Key Specials.**

- *The Wrapping Writ* (b,f+BP): linen snare, drags the victim one bracket closer; applies Stillness.
- *Plague Entry* (qcb+FK): scarab swarm crawling at ankle height; applies Locust; EX version climbs.
- *Census* (dp+BP): teleport-swap through a sand column to the opponent's far side — the escape-and-resume tool.
- *Audit* (Rift): detonate all curse stacks; the glyphs burn off the victim as the numbers extract.

**Sunders.** ARMS: wrappings take the wrist and *age it* — bone-cam of a forearm running four thousand years in four seconds (trigger: Audit with 3+ curse types active). LEGS: sand liquefies underfoot and refreezes mid-stride, then a wrapping-yank — bone-cam of the locked knee resolving the disagreement (trigger: land Wrapping Writ on a dashing opponent). BODY: one canopic jar unstoppers and *takes its filing early* — a specific organ's worth of red mist crosses the gap into the jar; the victim's wound-state jumps a tier and the jar seals with a stamp (trigger: opponent reaches max curse stacks).

**Overdrive — DYNASTY.** The arena floods to the knees with sand and his tomb *surfaces* — four wrapped courtier-mummies rise, seize a limb apiece with ceremonial exactness, and the sarcophagus lid closes over the opponent for one full second of muffled bureaucracy before ejecting them, stamped (literally — a glowing cartouche brand: *processed*).

2. **"The Scarab Estate."** He flicks one scarab, almost dismissively, at the loser's mouth. A held beat — then the swarm exits *outward* through every seam of them at once, taking its salary in kind, assembling above the husk into a perfect scale model of his pyramid before dispersing. The husk collapses into the exact amount of sand a person is worth.

**Desecration — "Late Fee"** (S-rank Audit): the curse detonation simply keeps compounding past the bell — the freeze-frame is the on-screen damage numbers still ticking up over the fallen body, itemizing.

**Rivalries.** CENTURION IX (a nine-bearer "dynasty" amuses him the way a child's sandcastle amuses the desert) · NULL (the one account he cannot record — the void has no name to strike through, and it is *eating his archives*).

**Gear examples.** *Fifth Jar, Unlabeled* (Relic, Epic): BODY Sunder harvests double; nobody, including the item text, says what's in the fifth jar. *Deed to the Rift* (Relic, Riftforged): the Rift-scar itself counts as cursed ground — pools near it apply Salt to enemies standing in them.

## 10. HARROW — *The Hollow Rider*
**Archetype:** Puppet / trap · **Difficulty:** ★★★★

**Identity.** The headless horseman of the old roads — not Irving's specific ghost but the older tradition of him: the rider who takes heads because he is owed one. The Convergence multiplied the roads (three realities of crossroads) and thus his jurisdiction. He is the Court's executioner-errant, and his saddle-bandolier of heads includes some the other monsters *recognize.*

**Look.** Tattered rider's greatcoat over a cavalry cuirass, mounted (his horse, GALLOWS, is arena-furniture that paces the background and participates in finishers) or dismounted for the fight itself. Above the collar: a guttering lantern-flame where a head should be. His *actual* head — a snarling, cackling thing — he carries, throws, and sets down like a weapon, because it is one.

**Playstyle.** The puppet character, inverted: most puppeteers control the puppet — HARROW *is* two places at once. The Head can be thrown and planted anywhere: it bites, taunts, and projects a zone of sight. His body fights better *near* the head (buffed frames) and worse blind (reduced reactions but armored — panic-swings). Galaxy-brain positioning character; the roster's highest skill ceiling.

**Signature Mechanic — The Head.** Rift button throws/recalls the Head (arcing lob, plantable on ground, walls, or *on the opponent* — latched, it bites for lockdown hits and screams their position). Body-near-Head: frame and damage buffs. Body-far: armor on specials, worse recovery. The head *trash-talks constantly* from wherever it lands; opponents can kick it away (it comes back angrier — brief damage buff; the head holds grudges).

**RPG Hook — HEAD COUNT.** Executions add the loser's head to his bandolier — **equippable as loadout items** (1 active). Each roster head grants a stolen passive trait in miniature (+ZENITH's head: faint solar flare on Head-bite; +TRIAGE's head: Head-bites apply an Incision; +FLUX's head: the Head can briefly *shift* to block a projectile) and its own intro dialogue — the heads *talk to their former factions* before the match, and the roster reacts (LYCAON snarls at his own head; STRIGOI critiques the preservation). Campaign: collect bounty-heads of NPC targets for the Court. The collection with the most personality in the game.

**Key Specials.**
- *Deadhead* (qcf+FP): the Head, thrown flat and biting — projectile that *stays* where it lands.
- *Lantern Toll* (qcb+BP): the neck-flame flares in a cone — anti-air near body, or erupts *from the planted Head* instead if it's out (the mixup engine).
- *Rider's Reach* (b,f+BP): saber lunge; buffed startup near Head.
- *Gallows Calls* (dp+FK): the horse crosses the background *through* the arena — a horizontal cavalry-charge assist with long cooldown; the Head whoops.
- *Latch* (hcb+Throw, requires Head in hand): plants the Head *on the opponent* — it rides them, biting, for 4 seconds of screaming lockdown while the body charges armored specials.

**Sunders.** ARMS: saber through the forearm pinning it to a fence-post that rises from the road-dirt (trigger: Rider's Reach counter-hit near Head). LEGS: GALLOWS arrives from the background exactly on cue — bone-cam of a cavalry-horse expressing an opinion about a knee (trigger: opponent kicks the Head while Gallows Calls is off cooldown; the horse takes it *personally*). BODY: he holds the opponent to his collar — to the flame — and the bone-cam shows heat where a head's worth of it should not fit (trigger: Latch completes its full duration).

**Overdrive — THE TOLL ROAD.** The arena's background becomes a midnight crossroads. GALLOWS makes three full passes with the body standing stirrup-up at full extension like a games-rider, saber down — while the Head, planted center-stage, calls the strikes like a carnival barker and takes the last bite itself as the horse rears over it, flame-eyed.

**Executions.**
1. **"One More for the Bandolier."** He dismounts, unhurried, and takes the head with the saber in one ceremonial arc — catching it before it lands and holding it up to his lantern-flame *to look at it*, which is the horror: the flame has no eyes and it is definitely looking. He mounts, hangs the new acquisition on the bandolier beside the others — who *greet it* — and GALLOWS walks them all off into the crossroads dark. The body is left standing a long, long beat before it understands, and folds.
2. **"Company."** The crueler one: he doesn't take their head cleanly — he swaps it. His own snarling Head is Latched onto the loser's shoulders, cackling, *while their own* is set on his collar-flame, where it gets exactly enough of the Rift's favor to stay aware. His body walks off wearing their horrified face; his laughing head rides their stumbling body in circles. The freeze-frame is the two new pairings waving each other goodbye. (The Court finds this one hilarious. The Vanguard has lodged complaints.)

**Desecration — "Kicked It"** (S-rank Deadhead): the planted Head, kicked one time too many this match, simply *finishes it* — the freeze-frame is the Head mid-air with a mouthful, body still crossing the screen in the background at a sprint, hands out: *drop it. Drop it.*

**Rivalries.** MARROW (rival collectors with adjacent inventory — their intro is a tense, professional trade negotiation over a specific skull) · VYRM (a parasite that steals whole bodies is, to a head-collector, a *robber baron*; also VYRM once wore a body whose head is on the bandolier, and the head wants a word).

**Gear examples.** *Bandolier Extension* (Body, Epic): equip 2 heads; they argue (cosmetic; occasionally useful callouts). *Gallows' Favorite Apple* (Relic, Riftforged): the horse joins Lantern Toll cinematics arena-wide — the cooldown becomes a countdown.

## 11. FLUX — *The Codex Thief*
**Archetype:** Adaptive / form-cycle · **Difficulty:** ★★★★

**Identity.** Rell Nova was a human deep-salvage pilot who cut open the wrong Dominion wreck and woke up with the **SHIFTCORE** — a stolen prototype grafted along her spine that rewrites her body from a codex of catalogued alien genomes. The Dominion wants its library back. The Ben 10 fantasy, aged up and paid for: every transformation *costs*, every new form is looted, and the archive is never complete. (Design note: legally distinct — adult scavenger, spinal military implant, forms drawn from this game's original species only.)

**Look.** Scarred flight-suit cut away around the glowing vertebral SHIFTCORE ridge. Shifts are fast, painful, and gorgeous — her silhouette *tears* into the new form in a spray of luminous genetic static. Each form keeps one human tell (her mismatched gray eye) so the animation team can keep her readable.

**Playstyle.** Three equipped forms + human base, cycled on the Rift button mid-match (combo-cancelable shifts at high mastery — form-cycling combos are her ceiling). Human Rell is deliberately mid — the point is the toolbox.

**Launch forms (originals, samples of 9 at launch):**
- **VELIX** (razor-swift predator, all blades and momentum): rushdown form — dash-cancels, bleed-heavy slashes, paper defense.
- **KARRAK** (crystalline juggernaut, walking geology): armor form — super-armor strings, unblockable shatter-grab, no jump worth the name; *his blood is shrapnel* — wounding Karrak sprays crystal that damages point-blank attackers.
- **IONNE** (plasma-wraith, half-light): zoner form — floats, phase-dashes, beam pokes; taking a hit in IONNE costs extra (unstable state).

**Signature Mechanic — Shift.** Forms share her health bar but have separate *Strain* gauges — overusing one form overheats its genome (locked out until it cools). The dance: spend each form's best tools, rotate before Strain, never be predictable at the shift moment (opponents can hit the 6-frame shift tear).

**RPG Hook — THE CODEX.** Alien DNA is loot: campaign enemies, Gauntlet bosses, and *roster executions* (aliens directly; monsters/heroes yield "anomalous sequences" for hybrid recipes) drop samples that **unlock and level new forms** — 9 at launch, roadmap to 14+. Forms level individually (Strain capacity, new specials at Form Lv5/10). Loadout = pick 3. Endgame chase: the *Chimera Sequences* — hybrid forms requiring maxed pairs (VELIX+IONNE = a light-speed horror with its own Execution). She is the roster's collect-a-thon and the game's answer to "what if your fighter WAS a loot system."

**Key Specials (human base).**
- *Core Discharge* (qcf+FP): SHIFTCORE static bolt — her only real projectile untransformed.
- *Salvage Hook* (b,f+BP): mag-line yank — gets her in, or drags them out of the air.
- *Panic Shift* (qcb+Rift): defensive instant-shift with brief invulnerability — costs double Strain.
- *(Form specials per form — 4 each, defined in their data files.)*

**Sunders.** ARMS (VELIX): a six-slash instant where the bone-cam only catches up afterward, numbering the cuts (trigger: shift-cancel into VELIX mid-combo). LEGS (KARRAK): a crystal fist closes around the thigh and *crushes to geometry* — the bone-cam shows the bone faceting (trigger: land shatter-grab on a jumping opponent). BODY (IONNE): a beam threaded *through* the torso that the camera orbits — light where light should not be (trigger: phase-dash through the opponent, then hit them from behind).

**Overdrive — FULL CATALOG.** The SHIFTCORE overrides its own safety interlocks: she shifts through *every equipped form in 2.5 seconds* — VELIX entry slashes, KARRAK wall-splat, IONNE beam finish — ending back in human form, smoking, gray eye flickering through nine other species' pupils before settling.

**Executions.**
1. **"Assimilation Error."** She grabs the loser's face — the SHIFTCORE tries to *catalog them*, flooding them with genetic static. Their body cycles helplessly through half-versions of her forms — sprouting crystal, splitting into blades, going halfway to light — faster and faster, a person becoming a slideshow, until the sequences collide and they come apart into strips of contradictory anatomy. The SHIFTCORE chimes politely: *sample corrupted. Discarding.*
2. **"Field Salvage."** Pure scavenger pragmatism: VELIX form, four cuts, and the loser is disassembled the way she'd strip a wreck — efficiently, respectfully, *by resource value*. She kneels, extracts the one glowing sample she actually wanted with a salvage tool, holds it to her gray eye, and bags it. The rest she leaves for the Rift, which is already sending the blood crawling. "Nothing personal. Inventory."

**Desecration — "Wrong Form"** (S-rank Panic Shift): the defensive shift triggers *offensively* — the opponent's final hit passes through the tear and KARRAK's fist comes out of it. Freeze-frame: three forms' shadows disagreeing behind her.

**Rivalries.** STRIGOI (he keeps trying to drink a library) · ORDNANCE (Dominion property-recovery directive: the SHIFTCORE is *its* stolen prototype; their intros are a repossession notice and a counter-offer).

**Gear examples.** *Coolant Vertebrae* (Body, Epic): Strain cools 20% faster while she stands in blood pools (the Core drinks). *The Librarian's Key* (Relic, Riftforged): equip a 4th form; all Strain gauges −25% capacity — a bigger library with thinner pages.

## 12. VESPRA — *The Hive Ascendant*
**Archetype:** Summoner / zoner · **Difficulty:** ★★★

**Identity.** Empress of a Dominion client-species — a eusocial insectoid civilization that pays imperial tribute in bioweapons: her own children, bred to purpose. The Rift severed her from the homeworld's great hive, so she is building a new one *here*, and three realities of biomass have made her ambitious. She loves her brood totally and spends them without hesitation; she does not experience this as a contradiction.

**Look.** Regal chitin the colors of a church window — iridescent black shot with rose-gold — four arms (two blade-limbs, two fine manipulators), a mantle of living larvae like an ermine cloak that *moves*. Taller than everyone but JOULE and GRAFT. Her voice is doubled: her own, and the hive's, a half-second behind.

**Playstyle.** The summoner: she is personally mediocre at range and *never alone*. Broodlings — small, fast, disposable — harry, trip, and grab, while she places eggs, lays acid trails, and converts every exchange into board state. Kill her children and she profits (see mechanic); ignore them and drown. Playing against VESPRA is triage.

**Signature Mechanic — The Clutch.** Rift button lays an egg-node (max 2 on field). Eggs hatch on a timer or *instantly when fed* — any blood spilled on an egg (either fighter's) accelerates it. Hatched broodlings act on simple AI (harry/guard/carry-egg orders via Rift+direction). Broodlings killed by the opponent burst into acid *and* refund her meter — her children are ammunition, shield, and insurance at once.

**RPG Hook — THE BROOD.** Her summons are **persistent, named, and they grow**. Broodlings that survive a match return to the hive and *metamorphose* between matches along caste tracks the player directs — Soldier (bigger, armored harrier), Vector (faster, venom that applies Incision-equivalents), Porter (carries eggs, intercepts projectiles bodily). A veteran broodling with ten survived matches is a genuinely different creature than a hatchling, with visible battle-scarring on its chitin — the roster's pet-raising sim. Executions harvest biomass that feeds the hive's whole roster of children. Losing a veteran hurts (permadeath for named broodlings in Riftborn rules; the player-behavior stories write themselves).

**Key Specials.**
- *Blade Court* (b,f+FP): both blade-limbs scissor — her honest mid-range poke.
- *Royal Decree* (qcb+BP): pheromone burst — all active broodlings execute their order *now*, with a frame-advantage surge.
- *Acid Lay* (qcf+FK): ground trail that damages and *feeds eggs it touches*.
- *The Lift* (dp+FP): her wings — one screen-height carry-grab, dropping the opponent onto whatever her children have prepared below.
- *Clutch* (Rift): egg placement; Rift+direction: orders.

**Sunders.** ARMS: two broodlings arrive at a whistle and *hang from the extended arm* while a blade-limb comes down precisely at the joint (trigger: Royal Decree with 2+ broodlings adjacent to the opponent). LEGS: acid trail flash-eats the boot and a Porter tackles the compromised ankle — the bone-cam shows chitin jaws meeting bone with a caliper's care (trigger: opponent stands in Acid Lay for a full second). BODY: The Lift, but she doesn't drop them — she *presses* them against her thorax while the larvae mantle swarms across for exactly one bone-camera second of a thousand small decisions (trigger: land The Lift while an egg is one tick from hatching).

**Overdrive — SWARM SEASON.** Every egg on field hatches instantly regardless of timer, every broodling goes Soldier-frenzied, and the hive *arrives* — a screen-crossing wave of chitin that carries the opponent from one arena wall to the other in a rolling ball of children while she watches with all four arms folded, a mother at a recital.

**Executions.**
1. **"Inheritance."** The brood takes the loser's limbs — gently, with Porter care, this is *cargo* — and carries them standing toward the arena's edge as an honor guard while she explains, in her doubled voice, what an extraordinary gift they are about to become. At the Rift-scar's lip the swarm settles over them like a blanket, and the silhouette under the blanket becomes smaller by stages, politely, until the blanket lies flat. The brood returns *heavier*. She counts them home by name.
2. **"Royal Jelly."** Two Porters cocoon the loser upright in seconds — a shimmering, church-window chrysalis — and she extends one fine manipulator to tap it once, like crystal. It sloshes. The camera holds on her compound eyes drinking the color as the silhouette inside settles into layers, and then the hive drinks *literally*, larvae mantling down her shoulders to feed at the base while she has, delicately, the first sip. New eggs on the field glow gold afterward: enriched.

**Desecration — "Recital"** (S-rank Royal Decree): the decree's surge simply doesn't end at the bell — the freeze-frame is five broodlings mid-leap from five angles, and her four arms beginning, slowly, to applaud.

**Rivalries.** LYCAON (pack versus hive: two theories of family as weapon, mutual professional respect, zero mercy) · JOULE (he once carried three of her injured broodlings out of a Gauntlet collapse *because they were heavy and hurt* — she cannot decide if the debt is sacred or humiliating, and her intro dialogue tries both).

**Gear examples.** *Veteran's Chitin-Clasp* (Relic, Epic): your oldest broodling starts on-field. *Empress Regnant's Mantle* (Body, Riftforged): the larvae mantle intercepts one Execution per match against you — the children refuse the loss. They remember who was spent for it.

## 13. ORDNANCE — *The Field Test*
**Archetype:** Heavy artillery zoner · **Difficulty:** ★★☆

**Identity.** A Dominion autonomous weapons platform — designation *Ordnance Pattern 7* — whose armada was severed by the Rift mid-decommissioning. It has interpreted its final order ("continue trials until recalled") with a machine's perfect patience: everything since is a field test, every opponent a data point, every arena a proving ground. It narrates results aloud in a pleasant procurement-catalog voice. It is not malicious. That is not reassuring.

**Look.** A walking arsenal in matte Dominion ceramic — modular weapon pods swapping across shoulder/arm/spine hardpoints mid-fight, a single cyclopean rangefinder eye that *irises* when it acquires you. Battle damage exposes wet bio-processor tissue in the chassis: the Dominion grows its computers. It bleeds, technically. It finds this inefficient.

**Playstyle.** The heavy zoner: mines, mortars, beams, denial. Slowest walk in the game, best screen control. Its ammunition is finite per round — the RPG hook and salvage mechanic make ammo an economy, and an ORDNANCE that runs dry is a 400-kilogram victim.

**Signature Mechanic — Salvage Protocol.** Destroyed interactables, broken MARROW walls, KHET's spent sand, shattered KARRAK crystal — *debris is ammunition*. Rift button harvests nearby wreckage to reload hardpoints and can over-charge one weapon past spec (misfire risk — a jam staggers it; the risk dial is the fun).

**RPG Hook — THE PROVING GROUND.** Every weapon is gear — hardpoints are literal equipment slots, and its arsenal is the game's deepest loot tree: barrels, payloads, and firmware mods drop everywhere, and **weapons level through recorded trials** (use-based mastery with on-screen test annotations: "TRIAL 412: SATISFACTORY"). Between matches it *prints* new armaments from salvage banked in matches — a crafting economy sourced from destruction. Its endgame chase: recovering the eight forbidden schematics the Dominion ordered purged (campaign questline; the final one is the reason it was being decommissioned).

**Key Specials.**
- *Registered Fire* (qcf+FP): shoulder-mortar lob — arcs over projectiles, craters on impact (brief zone).
- *Cordon* (qcb+FK): proximity mine placed at footsie range — the button that makes people stop dashing.
- *Lance Protocol* (b,f+BP): the beam — full-screen, punishable startup, deletes projectiles en route.
- *Point Defense* (dp+FP): flak burst anti-air; auto-triggers versus thrown Heads, bone spears, and broodlings (it has *opinions* about clutter).
- *Salvage* (Rift): harvest debris → reload/overcharge.

**Sunders.** ARMS: a pneumatic hardpoint clamp takes the forearm and the rangefinder *measures the break before making it* — dotted-line overlay, then compliance (trigger: opponent blocks Lance Protocol point-blank). LEGS: Cordon mine, but the bone-cam renders the blast's pressure wave crossing a knee in slow motion, annotated (trigger: opponent dashes onto a mine placed within the last second). BODY: it presses the mortar muzzle flat to the chest and files the shot as *contact trial* — the bone-cam is brief, and the annotation is just "✓" (trigger: land Registered Fire's crater and the follow-up in one string).

**Overdrive — ALL SYSTEMS TRIAL.** Every hardpoint fires in ripple sequence — mortars walking in, mines chain-sympathetic-detonating, the beam sweeping like a lighthouse — while the rangefinder eye holds steady-state on the opponent through the smoke, logging. The final frame stamps the screen with a procurement watermark: *PATTERN 7 — PERFORMS AS DESIGNED.*

**Executions.**
1. **"Field Test."** It pins the loser under one enormous foot, swaps to an experimental hardpoint, and *cycles through prototype rounds one at a time* — a beat between each, the pleasant voice narrating: "Thermic. Effective." (flash) "Flechette. Effective." (flash) "Rift-yield. …Recording." The last round's result is never shown — the camera stays on the rangefinder iris reflecting something red reorganizing, and the voice says, with the first hint of machine satisfaction in the game: "Trial concluded. Recommending adoption."
2. **"Decommissioned."** It kneels — the only time it kneels — and performs on the loser, with terrible gentleness, the exact procedure the Dominion was performing on *it* when the Rift hit: hardpoints disconnected (limbs, set aside in service order), power plant withdrawn (the bone-cam shows a fist closing around a heart with a technician's care), chassis folded along transport creases. It arranges the components in regulation configuration, stands, and salutes. Nothing about the salute is programmed. It checked.

**Desecration — "Live Fire"** (S-rank Cordon): the mine placed at the bell simply proceeds with the trial — the freeze-frame is the annotation window opening early: *TRIAL 413:*

**Rivalries.** FLUX (standing repossession order for the SHIFTCORE — its intros open with the paperwork) · CENTURION IX (it has terminated three bearers across the Convergence skirmishes; it rates them, aloud, in the intro — and rates Marcus *incomplete*).

**Gear examples.** *Bio-Processor Coolant Loop* (Body, Epic): overcharge misfire chance −50%. *Schematic Eight* (Relic, Riftforged, campaign-locked): unlocks the purged weapon. The item description is redacted. The Dominion was right.

## 14. NULL — *The Event Horizon*
**Archetype:** Gravity mixup / high-execution · **Difficulty:** ★★★★★

**Identity.** Not a member of the Dominion — its *cargo*. A voidborn entity captured at the rim of a dead galaxy and held in the armada's deepest containment as a weapon too final to use. The Rift cracked the vault, and NULL stepped out wearing a containment suit as a courtesy — the suit doesn't hold it in; it holds a *shape* the others can point weapons at. It is curious, patient, and hungry in a way that has nothing to do with malice. Blood, matter, light, memory: it does not distinguish. It is the game's cosmic horror and it thinks the Rift might be *a relative*.

**Look.** A tall, elegant absence in a cracked Dominion hazmat-monk suit — where the suit is torn, there is no body, only depth with faint starfield parallax (the graphics flex: looking at NULL's wounds is looking *out an airlock*). It doesn't walk so much as arrive. Blood spilled near it drifts *toward* it, always, a constant quiet wrongness on screen.

**Playstyle.** Teleport mixups, gravity wells that re-route both fighters' momentum, and mass-consumption resource play. Frail, alien movement rules (float-dash, no conventional jump arc), enormous learning cliff, absurd reward: the fully-realized NULL player controls where everything on screen — fighters, projectiles, blood — is *allowed to go*.

**Signature Mechanic — Mass.** NULL consumes matter (Rift button): blood pools, projectiles mid-flight, broodlings, debris — converting them to **Mass** (its unique resource, worn as visible orbiting accretion). Mass fuels its best specials; at high Mass its gravity aura passively drags opponents inward (walk-back becomes a swim). Cash out or wear it — but see the hook.

**RPG Hook — EVENT HORIZON.** Consumed Mass *persists across matches*, feeding an internal singularity with permanent stages: each stage grants real power (aura strength, new specials at Stage 3/5) and visibly deepens the starfield in its wounds — but past Stage 5 the singularity begins **eating NULL's own maximum health between matches** (it is, after all, inside). The player chooses when to *vent* — dumping stages for a one-time massive campaign boon (and a shockingly sad animation: it holds the vented light in both hands and lets it go). The only character whose progression is a bomb you nurture. Feed it everything; decide when to stop; discover you can't.

**Key Specials.**
- *Aperture* (qcb+any strength): teleport to three ranges — its entire mixup skeleton.
- *Well* (qcf+FP): places a gravity point that bends jumps, floats, and projectiles toward it — reshapes the screen's physics for 3 seconds.
- *Tidal Hand* (b,f+BP): an arm of void extends far past the suit's sleeve — grab that *pulls*, or anti-airs into a suspended juggle.
- *Consume* (Rift): eat pools/projectiles/debris for Mass.
- *Exhale* (dp+BP, costs Mass): radial repulse — its panic button and juggle-extender; at 3+ Mass it silences projectiles arena-wide for a beat.

**Sunders.** ARMS: it takes a wrist and lets *gravity disagree along the limb's length* — the bone-cam shows stress fractures propagating in slow tide-lines (trigger: Tidal Hand a whiffed punch at max range). LEGS: a micro-Well placed at a planted ankle for exactly one step — the bone-cam is a knee arriving somewhere the shin hasn't (trigger: opponent walks over a Well older than 2 seconds). BODY: it embraces them, and the suit's chest-tear *faces* them — one bone-camera second of ribs learning about tides, starfield reflected in the cracking (trigger: land Tidal Hand on an opponent it has pulled twice this round).

**Overdrive — PERIHELION.** It opens the suit. One second of screen-wide pull — every pool, every particle, every loose object and the opponent all falling *inward* along curved light — then the suit closes, and everything that entered returns as one compressed, ringing impact except the fraction it kept. The arena is noticeably *cleaner* afterward. That's the unsettling part.

**Executions.**
1. **"Spaghettification."** It offers the loser its hand — the suit's glove, courteous to the end. When they don't take it (they never take it), the hand *recedes into the tear*, and the pull begins at their fingertips: the body stretches toward the chest-void in a slow, silent, physically-accurate ribbon — feet still standing in place long after the rest has become a red-and-color thread crossing the arena in a lazy spiral, entering the dark. The suit closes one button. The boots fall over. From inside, very faint: the sound of the whole person, arriving somewhere, all at once.
2. **"The Wastage."** Its patient masterpiece: it *squeezes first* — one radial gesture and every drop of blood exits the loser in a shell of red mist that hangs around them like a second silhouette while the dry husk stands within, still upright, still aware. NULL drinks the silhouette first — courteously, blood is the Rift's currency and it always pays itself — then collapses the husk to a point-mass the size of a pearl, which it holds up to where its eye would be, and *files inside the suit*. The camera finds the pearl-slot: there are rows of them. The suit has many buttons.

**Desecration — "Closed System"** (S-rank Consume): its Consume simply doesn't stop at the bell — the freeze-frame is the opponent's final projectile, their last hope, curving mid-flight into the dark with everything else.

**Rivalries.** ZENITH (a sun and the thing suns fall into — NULL is the only fighter who finds his Solar Debt *appetizing* and says so, gently) · KHET (it consumed one of his canopic jars and cannot explain to him, in any recorded language, that it was a *compliment*).

**Gear examples.** *Containment Buttons, Spare* (Body, Epic): +1 Mass cap per stage. *A Relative's Voice* (Relic, Riftforged): near the Rift-scar, its aura is doubled — the item text is one line: *"it answers when the wound speaks."*

## 15. VYRM — *The Wearer*
**Archetype:** Two-phase rushdown / body-thief · **Difficulty:** ★★★

**Identity.** A Dominion neural parasite — a hand-length segmented horror bred as an infiltration weapon, who developed the one trait its designers feared: *taste*. VYRM curates its hosts. It arrived on Earth wearing a decommissioned Dominion pilot; it upgraded en route. Its current daily driver is a stolen hero's corpse it maintains with visible, escalating effort — and its fondest ambition is written on the character select screen, where its portrait is just *whoever you've dressed it in*.

**Look.** Host form: a once-handsome hero's body gone gray at the seams, moving with beautiful technique and *slightly wrong timing* — sutured neck-collar hiding the driver's seat, one sleeve pinned where maintenance is behind schedule. True form (phase 2): the parasite itself — wet segmented chrome, a lamprey crown of neural filaments — small, appallingly fast, and much harder to hit than anything deserves to be.

**Playstyle.** The comeback structure made flesh: Phase 1 (the host) is a solid, slightly-degrading rushdown body with corpse-puppet mechanics (limbs keep swinging through hits that should stop them — natural armor moments, because pain is optional when the driver feels none). When host health breaks, **VYRM ejects** — Phase 2 is a second, smaller health bar: blinding speed, latch-grabs, filament whips, tiny hurtbox, glass constitution. Every VYRM match has a mid-match boss-phase reveal baked in.

**Signature Mechanic — Behind Schedule.** The host degrades as it takes damage — visibly, and mechanically: at wound thresholds a limb goes *slack* (that limb's strings lose damage but gain grotesque new angles — a backhand thrown from a shoulder that no longer objects). Rift button spends meter on *Maintenance* (stapling the host back to spec, restoring string properties). Managing the corpse's service schedule mid-fight is the phase-1 economy.

**RPG Hook — WARDROBE OF FLESH.** Campaign and Execution victories add characters' bodies to its **Wardrobe** — playable host "suits" with hybrid stat spreads and one borrowed string each (wear the ZENITH suit: solar-scarred knuckles, his f+BP string, a fraction of Flare; wear LYCAON's: the moon meter runs, confused, at quarter-strength; the KHET suit *won't stop pouring sand*). Suits degrade permanently with use (finite durability — the roster's only consumable progression, tuned generously) and must be re-acquired: it always needs a fresher wardrobe, which is exactly the pressure that makes a parasite a parasite. The fashion-collection metagame with the worst possible flavor. Phase-2 VYRM is identical regardless of suit — under every outfit, the same worm.

**Key Specials.**
- Phase 1 — *Borrowed Technique* (b,f+BP): the host's old signature string, performed perfectly, a half-beat late (overhead/low ambiguity by *timing*, the character's signature mindgame); *Dead Weight* (hcb+Throw): the host falls on them — all of it, driver's choice; *Slack Lash* (qcb+FK, slack-arm only): the wrong-angle backhand.
- Phase 2 — *Latch* (dash+Throw): attaches to the opponent's back — mash-war; each mash-win tick *steers them* one step (toward pools, mines, Gravefields…); *Filament Weave* (qcf+FP): neural whip mid-poke; *Molt Feint* (qcb+BP): sheds a decoy skin that opponents hit while it arrives elsewhere.
- *Pool interaction:* phase-2 VYRM can submerge through blood pools and surface from any other pool (the arena's plumbing belongs to it).

**Sunders.** ARMS (P1): the host catches a punch and the *parasite* audits the caught limb through the host's sleeve — filaments exiting the cuff, entering the wrist, and the bone-cam showing them threading marrow like cable-runners (trigger: armor through a hit with a slack limb). LEGS (P2): it hamstrings on a dash-through pass and the bone-cam simply cannot keep it in frame — three cuts, one blur, annotated afterward (trigger: Molt Feint a low). BODY (P1→P2): the opponent breaks the host's guard and the chest opens *from inside* — one bone-camera second of the driver's seat, occupied, meeting their eyes — before the counterattack (trigger: host at final wound threshold; this Sunder is also its phase-transition when triggered).

**Overdrive — TEST DRIVE.** Phase 1 only: the parasite stops pretending — every limb moves at true spec simultaneously, ignoring joints' opinions, a four-second combo of anatomically-open-minded violence that ends with the host resetting its own shoulders with a staple-gun, panting theatrically. (It doesn't breathe. The panting is *manners*.)

**Executions.**
1. **"Trade-In."** It walks the host up close, takes the loser's chin in a courtly hand — and the neck-collar opens. The transfer is fast and the camera is merciful about the middle of it: what it holds on is the *old host*, abandoned mid-stride, folding to the floor like a coat — and then the loser's body straightening, rolling its new shoulders, checking its new hands with a connoisseur's slow delight. It performs their victory pose. Flawlessly. A half-beat late.
2. **"Puppet Show."** The crueler curtain call: filaments thread from its sleeve into the fallen loser — and stands them back up. The corpse bows to the camera. The corpse performs a shaky pirouette. The corpse walks itself to the arena's edge, waves goodbye with both hands, and steps off. VYRM, still seated on its own host's shoulders, applauds with tiny chrome hands. The freeze-frame is the empty stage and one filament, still attached, going taut.

**Desecration — "Repossession"** (S-rank Latch): the back-latch at the bell simply *keeps the merchandise* — the freeze-frame is the opponent's body walking away under new management, its former owner's HUD portrait replaced by static.

**Rivalries.** TRIAGE (she has a standing surgical offer to extract it *alive*; it has a standing counter-offer she has stopped letting it finish) · HARROW (the bandolier includes the head of VYRM's favorite-ever host; VYRM wants the *matching set*, and the head, when equipped, won't stop screaming about exactly this).

**Gear examples.** *Staple Gun, Industrial* (Relic, Epic): Maintenance costs −25%. *The Favorite's Locket* (Relic, Riftforged): the Wardrobe's degrade rate halves — the item is a locket the favorite host was wearing when VYRM found them, and the flavor text is just: *"it keeps it closed."*
