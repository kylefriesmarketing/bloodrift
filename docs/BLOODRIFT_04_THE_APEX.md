# BLOODRIFT — THE APEX
### Faction IV: the Villains — design addendum to BLOODRIFT_01/02
*Written 2026-08-05. The Convergence braided three worlds. It did not braid four — the
fourth was already here, and it had already won.*

---

## 1. Why this faction exists

The original three factions leave a hole you can feel: the Vanguard "buried most of their
roster the day of the Convergence," and nobody in the game put them in the ground. Earth
had heroes. Earth therefore had the people who beat them.

**THE APEX (Villains)** — *the world was already ours.*

On the morning of the Convergence, the Apex were **winning**. Not scheming, not monologuing:
winning. The Vanguard's dead were their work, hours old. Then a hole opened in causality and
dumped two more realities into their board game, and their victory — earned, paid for,
finished — was buried under monsters and aliens who had never heard of them.

They are not here to conquer the Rift. They are here to make it **give the win back**.

**Theme:** *the interrupted victory.* Where the Vanguard fights out of duty and the Court
out of appetite and the Dominion out of exile, the Apex fight out of **entitlement**. They
had it. It's theirs. Every duel at a Rift-scar is a hostile takeover.

**Visual identity:** expensive violence. Tailored charcoal and oil-black over powered plate,
gold seams, trophy-taking. Where the Vanguard is field-repaired and the Court is rotted
opulence, the Apex is *immaculate* — and the running joke of the art direction is that they
stay clean about a round longer than anyone else, and are the most horrifying when they don't.

**Faction colour:** oil-black + hard gold. **Faction branch:** *Dominion* is taken — the
Apex shared skill branch is **AVARICE**.

---

## 2. The four-way war (updated story spine)

Three realities collided; four powers want the Rift.

| Faction | Wants the Rift to… | Fights because |
|---|---|---|
| **THE VANGUARD** (heroes) | close, whatever it costs them | somebody has to hold the line, and there's nobody left |
| **THE APEX** (villains) | pay out — give back the world they'd won | it was *theirs*, and it was taken mid-sentence |
| **THE MIDNIGHT COURT** (monsters) | stay open forever | it runs on blood, and they have always run on blood |
| **THE SPIRAL DOMINION** (aliens) | reopen the way home — or crown them here | they are castaways with an armada's worth of grudge |

The Rift keeps score in blood and does not care who spills it. That's the joke the setting is
built on: four mutually exclusive victory conditions, one referee, and the referee is hungry.

**What the Apex changes about the campaign:** the Vanguard act is no longer heroes-vs-monsters.
It is heroes fighting the people who already beat them, *while* monsters and aliens use the
wreckage. And the Apex act is the game's most uncomfortable one — you play the people who
were right about how the world works.

---

## 3. The roster (5)

Design rule kept from the bible: **every fighter has one persistent mechanic no other fighter
has**, and no two share an archetype shape.

| Fighter | Archetype | Signature (in-match) | RPG Hook (persistent) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SOVEREIGN** | mastermind / counter-charge | **Contingency** — pre-loaded plans, not improvisation | **The Holdings** — defeated fighters become assets paying permanent dividends |
| **TERMINUS** | juggernaut / escalation | **The Hour** — grows stronger every ten seconds the round survives | **The Countdown** — carries escalation between matches; venting it heals |
| **HALFLIGHT** | dual-moveset rushdown | **Two Faces** — still owns her hero moveset *and* her villain one | **The Confession** — Execute/Spare permanently erodes one half. Execute enough and the hero is gone for good |
| **CHORUS** | telepath / debuffer | **Intrusion** — whispers that corrupt the victim's inputs | **Known** — learns your most-used move account-wide and punishes it |
| **KESTREL** | mercenary / rushdown | **Contract** — names a condition at round start; fulfilling it pays out | **The Fee** — banked contract money buys permanent upgrades; failures cost |

## 4. SOVEREIGN — *The Man Who Won*
**Archetype:** Mastermind / counter-charge · **Difficulty:** ★★★☆☆

**Identity.** Alaric Vane owned eleven percent of the planet and all of its worst ideas. He
did not hate the Vanguard; he *out-competed* them, and on Convergence morning he stood over
the last of them with the paperwork already filed. He has never raised his voice. He has
never run. He is the only person in three realities who considers the Rift a **breach of
contract**.

**Look.** Charcoal three-piece over black powered plate, gold seams tracing the load paths.
A hairline circuit-crown at the temple. Immaculate — and the art direction rule is that he
takes visible damage *last*, so the first blood on that suit reads as an event.

**Playstyle.** Punishes impatience. Charge specials, a converting parry, screen control. He
does not open you up; he waits for you to open yourself and then bills you for it.

**Signature — CONTINGENCY.** He loads **two plans** before the round. The Rift button
executes the next one in the queue: *Counter-Clause* (parry stance that converts to a
command grab), *Foreclosure* (teleport-strike to the opponent's back), *Indemnity* (one-hit
armour barrier). Prepared, never improvised — and once a plan is spent it's gone for the round.

**RPG Hook — THE HOLDINGS.** Every character he defeats is **acquired**. Each holding pays a
permanent passive dividend (+1% damage, +2% meter rate, a shortened charge…), and every third
acquisition unlocks a **board seat** — a third, then fourth Contingency slot. The endgame is
owning the entire roster: at 19 holdings he opens the round with every plan loaded, which is
the closest this game has to a stated final boss for a *player*.

**Key Specials.** *Margin Call* (charge b~f+BP): armoured advancing backhand · *Due Diligence*
(qcb+BP): parry that converts into a throw on success · *Liquidate* (qcf+FP): a lance of
hard-light billed to the arena · *Golden Parachute* (dp+FK): rising escape kick, invincible
startup · *Contingency* (RIFT): execute the next loaded plan.

**Sunders.** ARMS: he takes the wrist, reads the watch, and breaks the arm at the hour hand
(trigger: parry with Due Diligence). LEGS: Margin Call at the knee, and he keeps walking
(trigger: armour through a hit, then punish). BODY: he presses two fingers to the sternum and
*Forecloses* — the bone-cam shows ribs giving way in the shape of a hand (trigger: spend both
Contingencies in one round).

**Overdrive — HOSTILE TAKEOVER.** The arena's lights come up like a boardroom. Eleven seconds
of documented, itemised violence delivered by a man who does not loosen his tie, ending with
the opponent seated in a chair that rose from the floor while he signs something and slides it
across. The stamp is the hit.

**Executions.**
1. **"Fully Vested."** He shakes their hand. The gold seams run *up their arm*, and what
   walks away from the handshake is him, wearing their silhouette for exactly one second
   before it sloughs off — a demonstration, not a transformation. The husk keeps standing
   because nobody told it it could stop.
2. **"Terms."** He hands them a page. They read it. The camera never shows the page. Whatever
   it says, they do it to themselves, methodically, while he checks his watch — and the horror
   is that he doesn't watch. He's already looking at the next opponent.

**Desecration — "Called In"** (S-rank Margin Call): the armoured backhand simply continues
past the bell; the freeze-frame is his hand out, palm up, waiting to be paid.

**Rivalries.** ZENITH (he buried the Vanguard and considers Zenith an *unclosed account*) ·
KHET (two archivists of owed things; the pharaoh finds him vulgar and *efficient*) ·
CENTURION IX (Alaric bought the Armory's missing pieces decades ago — Marcus is chasing
relics that are already on a shelf in Vane's office).

## 5. TERMINUS — *The Long Hour*
**Archetype:** Juggernaut / escalation · **Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆

**Identity.** Nobody knows what Terminus was. What is documented: in 1961 a containment event
in the Nevada desert produced a man-shaped thing that has not stopped since, and every year it
is measurably *harder to stop*. It does not speak. It does not hurry. Every fight it has ever
been in ended the same way — not because it won early, but because it was still standing when
everything else wasn't. The Rift did not impress it.

**Look.** Slab-shouldered, sixties-industrial: riveted plate over something that isn't quite a
body, cooling fins along the spine that come alive as it escalates — first amber, then white.
By late in a round it is visibly *venting*, and the arena air distorts around it.

**Playstyle.** The clock is its weapon. Slow, armoured, enormous reach. Kill it early or
explain to yourself why you didn't.

**Signature — THE HOUR.** An escalation gauge ticks up **every ten seconds the round lasts**,
and on every wound it takes. Each stage grants armour on more moves, +damage, and at stage 4
its walk becomes an unstoppable advance. Escalation resets on round end — but see the hook.

**RPG Hook — THE COUNTDOWN.** A fraction of the escalation reached **persists into the next
match**, and keeps compounding across a session: the longer your fights run, the more
dangerous your Terminus starts. Two branches: *Patience* (higher carry, slower gain) vs
*Detonation* (spend the whole gauge for one catastrophic minute, then start from zero, hurt).
Campaign rest nodes offer to **vent** — heal fully, lose the countdown. It is always the
wrong choice and always the safe one.

**Key Specials.** *Grind* (b,f+BP): armoured walking shoulder · *Sundown* (qcb+FK): a
delayed ground fissure that arrives when *it* decides · *Press* (hcb+Throw): it simply leans
on you · *Overpressure* (dp+BP): vents heat as an anti-air column · *The Hour* (RIFT): manual
overheat — force one stage now, take chip to do it.

**Sunders.** ARMS: it closes a hand around the forearm and *waits* (trigger: reach stage 3).
LEGS: the walking shoulder does not stop at the leg (trigger: land Grind during an advance).
BODY: Press, held, while the escalation ticks over on-screen (trigger: land Press at stage 4).

**Overdrive — TERMINAL VELOCITY.** Every vent opens at once. It walks — just walks — through
the entire arena, and the camera stays behind it, and whatever was in the way is behind it too.

**Executions.**
1. **"Long Hour."** It sets them down, sits on the arena floor facing them, and waits. The
   round timer appears and runs. It does nothing. The loser tries to crawl. The timer hits
   zero and so do they, and the thing gets up and dusts its knees, and this is the only
   Execution in the game where the winner never touches the loser at all.
2. **"Overpressure."** It embraces them and opens every vent inward. The bone-cam is one
   frame of a person becoming a pressure vessel, and then the arena is very bright and very
   quiet and there is a shadow on the wall behind where it stood.

**Desecration — "Still Standing"** (S-rank Grind): the advance continues past the bell; the
freeze-frame is the far arena wall.

**Rivalries.** JOULE (two men who profit from being hit — Dez finds it genuinely sad, which
enrages nobody, which is worse) · NULL (a thing that grows versus a thing that consumes;
they have fought for nine hours before and neither noticed the sun) · TERMINUS/CENTURION IX
(it killed the Third Bearer in 1974; the Armory remembers).

## 6. HALFLIGHT — *The Turned*
**Archetype:** Dual-moveset rushdown · **Difficulty:** ★★★★☆

**Identity.** Ione Reyes was Vanguard. She has the medal. She also has the reason, and the
reason is that on Convergence Day the Vanguard made a call — a defensible, arithmetic,
*correct* call — that cost her the people she'd have died for, and she has decided that being
right is not the same as being good. She fights with a hero's technique and a villain's
follow-through, and the awful thing is how little she had to change.

**Look.** Half a Vanguard uniform, still clean, still the old colours on one side. The other
side is what she's built since: Apex black, gold seam, no insignia. Split down the centre,
literally — and her two halves animate slightly differently, the hero side a half-beat more
disciplined.

**Playstyle.** Two complete movesets, both *hers*, swapped on the Rift button — but unlike
FLUX (who is collecting forms) or LYCAON (who is on a calendar), Halflight's swap costs
**conviction**: each switch spends a little of the gauge that decides who she becomes.

**Signature — TWO FACES.** RIFT swaps between **BRIGHT** (Vanguard: parries, disciplined
pokes, a reversal that respects the rules) and **DARK** (Apex: cancels, mix-ups, moves that
punish a trusting opponent). Swapping is free once per round; every swap after costs meter and
tilts the Confession.

**RPG Hook — THE CONFESSION.** Her two halves **diverge permanently based on your Execute /
Spare record.** Execute, and Dark moves gain properties while Bright ones are *pruned* —
literally removed from her list, one at a time. Spare, and the reverse. Take it far enough
either way and one moveset is **gone for good on that profile**, and her portrait, intro, and
Overdrive change to match. The only fighter whose kit can be permanently and irreversibly
reduced by how you play. The bible's Execute/Spare economy, aimed at the player.

**Key Specials.** BRIGHT — *Oath* (qcb+BP): parry that refuses to counter-hit a downed foe ·
*Rally* (dp+FP): rising uppercut, invincible, the old Vanguard reversal. DARK — *Recant*
(qcf+FP): a thrown medal, edge-first · *Attrition* (b,f+BP): a low-committing runner that
crosses under · *Answer* (hcb+Throw): the technique she was taught, used the way she was
taught not to.

**Sunders.** ARMS (BRIGHT): a textbook disarm taken two degrees past textbook (trigger: Oath
parry). LEGS (DARK): Attrition, through the knee, from behind (trigger: cross-under, then hit).
BODY: she uses the Vanguard's own medical hold — the one taught for *saving* somebody — and
does not let go (trigger: swap faces mid-combo).

**Overdrive — CONVICTION.** Both halves at once for four seconds, a combo that alternates
hero and villain technique, and the finish depends on the Confession: BRIGHT ends with her
catching them; DARK ends with her not.

**Executions.**
1. **"The Call."** She restages it: she puts them exactly where her people were, and gives
   them the same defensible, arithmetic, correct choice — and then makes the decision the
   Vanguard made, out loud, in their voice. The bone-cam here is a memory, not a body.
2. **"Honourably."** She kills them the *right* way. Clean, fast, respectful, by the book —
   the book she still knows by heart. She closes their eyes. Then she takes the medal.

**Desecration — "Recanted"** (S-rank Recant): the thrown medal doesn't knock them down, it
goes *through*; the freeze-frame is the medal spinning on the floor and her already walking.

**Rivalries.** ZENITH (he trained her; he has not said her name since) · TRIAGE (Imara
signed the medical order that made the call defensible — the one person Halflight cannot
look at) · SOVEREIGN (he did not recruit her; she *applied*, and he finds that fascinating).

## 7. CHORUS — *The Many-Mouthed*
**Archetype:** Telepath / debuffer · **Difficulty:** ★★★★☆

**Identity.** She was a chorus teacher. Then a Rift-scar opened under a concert hall during a
performance and four hundred voices went in and one thing came out, and it is polite, and it
remembers all their names, and it is very interested in what you were about to do. She does
not read minds. She *edits* them.

**Look.** A slight woman in a conductor's black, but the silhouette is wrong: too many
shoulders, and the shadow behind her has a choir's worth of heads. When she speaks the
subtitles arrive slightly before the audio.

**Playstyle.** The most invasive debuffer in the game. She doesn't out-damage you, she makes
you unreliable to yourself — and then punishes what you do instead.

**Signature — INTRUSION.** Her attacks plant **whispers**. A whisper doesn't damage; it
corrupts the victim's *inputs* for a moment: a delayed button, a swapped direction, a dropped
special. Stacked whispers escalate from a stumble to a genuine loss of the controller for a
beat. The Rift button **conducts** — detonating every whisper at once into a hitstun chorus.
(Design guardrail: whisper windows are short, telegraphed by an audio cue and a HUD flicker,
and never fire during a punish window — the goal is dread, not unfairness.)

**RPG Hook — KNOWN.** She *learns you*. Account-wide, per character, she records the move you
use most and permanently gains bonus damage and counter-hit priority against it. Fight her
often with the same habit and she starts *anticipating* it — her intro line names the move.
The counterplay is to change how you play, which is the only counterplay she respects. Her
tree deepens either **Repertoire** (more tracked habits) or **Solo** (one habit, punished
catastrophically).

**Key Specials.** *Descant* (qcf+FP): a thrown note that plants a whisper on contact ·
*Round* (qcb+FK): a low, looping phrase that hits twice · *Fermata* (dp+BP): she holds a
note — anti-air that freezes the opponent's inputs at the peak · *Cue* (hcb+Throw): she takes
their jaw and gives them a line to say · *Conduct* (RIFT): detonate all whispers.

**Sunders.** ARMS: she asks the arm to stop belonging to them and it agrees (trigger: Conduct
with 3+ whispers). LEGS: mid-stride she changes which leg they think is forward — bone-cam of
the disagreement (trigger: whisper an opponent during a dash). BODY: four hundred voices at
once, at range, and the bone-cam is the ribcage resonating apart (trigger: land Cue on a
whispered opponent).

**Overdrive — TUTTI.** The arena fills with the choir — four hundred half-there figures in
concert dress — and they *sing the opponent's name*, and the opponent's own body performs the
piece. She conducts. It is the only Overdrive in the game where the victim does all the work.

**Executions.**
1. **"Encore."** She asks them to sing. They do — beautifully, in a voice that is not theirs
   and never was — and they keep going after they should have stopped, and after they should
   have been able to, and the last note holds long past the body's ability to hold it.
2. **"Four Hundred."** She simply lets the others have a turn. The camera stays on her face,
   pleasant, attentive, occasionally correcting a tempo, while behind her the sound does
   something the visuals never show.

**Desecration — "Perfect Pitch"** (S-rank Descant): the note lands and the opponent's HUD
portrait *joins the choir*; the freeze-frame is the portrait singing.

**Rivalries.** HARROW (a collector of heads meets a collector of voices; his bandolier
*hums* when she's near and he hates it) · FLUX (a mind with nine bodies versus a body with
four hundred minds) · VYRM (they have the same business model and Chorus finds the parasite's
version *crude*).

## 8. KESTREL — *The Fee*
**Archetype:** Mercenary / rushdown · **Difficulty:** ★★☆☆☆

**Identity.** She is not ideological, which in this company makes her the most reasonable
person in the room and the least trustworthy. Kestrel takes contracts. She took one against
the Vanguard on Convergence morning and completed it forty minutes before reality broke, and
she has never once been paid, and she would like the Rift to understand that this is a
**billing matter**.

**Look.** Practical: layered flight-leathers, a half-mask, a spool of monofilament at the hip
and a lot of small, well-kept knives. The only Apex fighter who looks like she could leave.
Gold only on the buckles — she works for these people, she isn't one of them.

**Playstyle.** The roster's fastest honest rushdown: no gimmick movement, just superior speed,
frame traps and a punishing okizeme game. New players can win with her; good players can win
*differently* with her.

**Signature — CONTRACT.** At the start of each round she **names a condition** on screen —
"land eight hits", "win without blocking", "no jumps", "finish under thirty seconds". Fulfil
it and she's **paid**: an immediate power spike for the rest of the round (damage, speed,
a free meter bar). Fail it and nothing bad happens *in the round* — but see the hook. The
opponent can read the contract too, which makes it a two-way mind game: you know what she's
trying to do, and stopping her costs you tempo.

**RPG Hook — THE FEE.** Completed contracts bank **coin** that buys permanent upgrades from a
list only she can access (faster walk, an extra frame of invuln on wake-up, a fourth string
ender). Failed contracts **cost** coin. She is the only fighter whose progression can go
backwards, and the only one where the player is invited to take a bad deal on purpose because
the payout is worth it. Her tree: **Retainer** (safe, small, guaranteed) vs **Bounty** (harder
contracts, obscene payouts).

**Key Specials.** *Invoice* (qcf+FK): a thrown knife that sticks and pays interest on the next
hit · *Collections* (b,f+FP): a low dash-in that crosses up on a whiff · *Severance*
(hcb+Throw): the monofilament, used the way monofilament is used · *Kite* (qcb+BP): a
grappling retreat that reverses her spacing entirely · *Terms* (RIFT): re-roll the contract,
once per round, for a worse one with a better payout.

**Sunders.** ARMS: monofilament takes the arm at a joint she picked earlier in the fight —
the bone-cam shows the earlier cut she left as a guide (trigger: complete a contract). LEGS:
Collections at the ankle, from behind, on a whiff (trigger: punish a whiffed special). BODY:
she gets *inside* the guard and the camera stays close and businesslike (trigger: land
Severance on a contracted target).

**Overdrive — PAID IN FULL.** She produces an itemised bill, and then collects it, once per
line, at a speed the camera has to slow down to explain, and the last line is always the
same and the camera lets you read it.

**Executions.**
1. **"Invoiced."** She kills them efficiently and without cruelty — and then, and this is
   the part the Court finds hilarious, she goes through their effects, takes exactly what she
   is owed, counts it, and leaves the rest. She has never once taken more.
2. **"Kited."** The monofilament goes around them and around the nearest architecture, and
   she walks away holding the other end, and the arena does the rest as she takes up the
   slack. She does not look back; she's already reading the next contract.

**Desecration — "Overdue"** (S-rank Invoice): the thrown knife lands and simply keeps
collecting past the bell; the freeze-frame is a receipt.

**Rivalries.** MARROW (Sera's masterwork list has a standing order for monofilament and
Kestrel *has* some, and neither will name a price) · GRAFT (he has offered to pay her in
parts; she has explained, patiently, that she takes coin) · SOVEREIGN (she has never been
paid and he has never been billed; every intro is the same forty-second conversation).

Existing fighters gain a fourth-faction line: ZENITH ↔ SOVEREIGN is now the roster's
**central rivalry** — the dying sun and the man who filed the paperwork on him.

## 10. What this changes elsewhere

- **Campaign** becomes four acts, not three. The Apex act is the shortest and the meanest.
- **CONFLUX** (the final boss) now assembles from four factions' silhouettes; its Apex
  contribution is the only part of it wearing a *suit*, and it is the part that speaks.
- **Arenas**: the Apex home stage is **The Vane Atrium** — a boardroom the size of a cathedral,
  glass floor over the Rift-scar, with a table nobody is sitting at.
- **Tempered Mode** treats Apex hooks like every other faction's: match-scoped only. SOVEREIGN
  keeps his contingencies, loses his holdings; HALFLIGHT gets both movesets intact.
